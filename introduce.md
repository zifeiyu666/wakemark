## 为什么创建
### 版本1
Every day, we encounter high-signal thinking across the web: a clever architecture pattern, a late-night debugging fix, or an actionable breakdown of modern AI engineering. We hit “Bookmark” almost instinctively, trusting that our future selves will return to it.

The reality? Bookmarking is where great insights go to die.

Traditional tools demand tedious manual sorting—endless folders and arbitrary tags that quickly turn into yet another maintenance chore. Meanwhile, native search on social platforms is notoriously frustrating, making it nearly impossible to retrieve that one critical thread when you only remember a vague phrase or an approximate timeline.

We built Wakemark with a simple premise: turn your passive saves into an active, conversational knowledge asset.

With AI-native semantic embeddings and natural language chat, organization happens without effort. You no longer need to remember exact authors or exact phrasing—just ask your bookmark library the way you would ask a peer. More importantly, native MCP (Model Context Protocol) integration allows tools like Cursor and Claude to query your saved library directly as contextual memory while you write code.

Your bookmarks shouldn’t sit idle in a digital drawer. Wakemark wakes them up so they actually work for you.
### 版本2
As a developer constantly browsing X, Reddit, and technical communities, I have a persistent habit: I bookmark everything. Hardcore architecture breakdowns, subtle frontend quirks, useful CLI tips, and new AI tools—if it sparks an idea, I save it.

Over time, that turned into thousands of saved posts. But whenever an actual engineering roadblock hit and I vaguely remembered seeing an answer weeks ago, finding it was excruciating. No fuzzy recall, clumsy filtering, and zero programmatic access. Existing tools were either expensive platforms designed primarily for long-form books or outdated web clippers stuck in the manual folder era.

So I built Wakemark—first for myself, and now for anyone who treats social content as a learning canvas.

Wakemark isn’t another passive “read-it-later” bucket. It is an active second brain for your saved web:

Frictionless Sync & Auto-Categorization: Imports your bookmarks automatically, generating multi-dimensional semantic tags and clean summaries in the background.

Semantic Recall: Query your bookmarks with natural language—ask about "that Next.js Server Action workaround from last week" and pull up the exact thread instantly.

Native MCP Integration: Bridges directly into your developer workflow, letting Cursor and Claude treat your curated bookmarks as real-time context when coding or brainstorming.

Bookmarks shouldn't be the final resting place of great ideas—they should be the starting point. Excited to hear your thoughts and feedback!
